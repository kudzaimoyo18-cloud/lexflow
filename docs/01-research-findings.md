# LexFlow — Research Findings (June 2026)

Working name: **LexFlow**. Target: solo + small law firms (1–10 lawyers). Market: global-first (jurisdiction-neutral core), validated against US/UK data because that is where the research exists.

## 1. The core economics problem

The average lawyer bills only **2.9 hours of an 8-hour day** (~33% utilization). Of time actually worked, industry conversion is roughly **37% utilization x 88% realization x 91% collection = ~29.6% of working time becomes collected revenue**. Small/mid firms average ~83% realization.

Translation: a small firm's biggest competitor is not another firm — it is its own admin overhead. Every product decision should attack one of three leaks:

| Leak | Evidence | Annual cost (typical small firm) |
|------|----------|----------------------------------|
| Lost leads at intake | 35% of inquiries never get any response; avg web-form response is 17–42 hours; conversion drops 72% if response > 5 min; 79% of clients sign with the first firm that responds | $200,000+ in unanswered calls alone (multi-attorney firm) |
| Unbilled / written-off time | Realization avg 88% (small firms 83%); missing time entries + write-offs; 90-day median lockup ties up ~3 months of revenue | 10–17% of all worked time evaporates |
| Document drudgery | Lawyers report 40–60% of the day drafting/reviewing documents; doc management cited by 58% as biggest challenge | Largest time sink, least direct revenue link |

## 2. Pain points ranked (pain x automatability x willingness to pay)

1. **Client intake & lead response** — extreme pain, fully automatable (AI receptionist, forms, qualification, scheduling, engagement letters, e-sign, retainer payment). Direct "we make you money" sales story. **Winner.**
2. **Time capture & billing** — passive time tracking, auto-drafted invoices, collections follow-up. Strong ROI story ("realization +10% = +12.5% capacity"). Natural expansion module.
3. **Document drafting/automation** — biggest time sink but crowded (Spellbook, Gavel, Harvey) and harder to do jurisdiction-neutral. Phase 3.
4. **Deadlines/calendaring & matter management** — table stakes; 77% of lawyers still run tasks via email. Must exist but not the wedge.
5. **E-discovery / research** — enterprise territory, skip.

## 3. Competitive landscape

| Player | Position | Gap we exploit |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| Clio | Market leader, $39–139/user/mo but real cost ~$138+/user with workflow add-ons | Pricing creep, declining support, AI bolted-on not native |
| MyCase | $39–99/user/mo | Weak document automation, weak integrations, mobile gaps |
| Smokeball | Hybrid desktop, opaque pricing | Not cloud-native |
| Lawmatics | Intake/CRM only | No matter management or billing; firms need 2nd tool |
| Spellbook / Gavel | AI contract work in Word, $160+/mo, 6-month commitments | Priced/structured for transactional teams, not solo GP firms |
| Harvey | $1,000+/seat enterprise, $11B valuation | Explicitly not for small firms |

**Structural gap:** Nobody owns "AI-native, all-in-one for solo/small, flat simple pricing." Incumbents have AI as add-on; AI-natives are point solutions or enterprise. 41% of firms cite fragmented tools as their top pain — they are stitching Clio + Lawmatics + Spellbook + RingCentral.

## 4. Market size & timing

- Legal tech: $29.8B (2025) -> ~$65.5B (2034); ~$6B VC into legal tech in 2025.
- Tech *selection* fatigue is now lawyers' #1 challenge (54%) — they want one tool that works, not another point solution.
- 76% of legal staff cling to manual processes when tools do not integrate — onboarding simplicity is a feature, not polish.

## 5. The wedge decision

**V1 wedge: the Revenue Front Door — automated intake -> engagement -> retainer.**

Why intake beats documents and billing as the wedge:
- It is the only pain with a *make money* story instead of *save time* (easier sale, provable ROI within weeks: "you closed N clients you would have missed").
- Fully automatable today with current AI; no jurisdiction-specific law needed (forms, conflict checks, engagement letters are firm-configurable templates).
- Lawmatics proved demand but stops at CRM; we continue into matter + billing, killing the fragmentation pain.

Expansion path: Intake (v1) -> passive time capture + billing/collections (v2) -> AI document drafting (v3). Each module sells to the same buyer and deepens lock-in.
